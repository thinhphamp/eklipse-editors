Captions tab: three ways to tweak it

Production already asks for language, caption format and auto emoji before generating, and edits captions as lines with time ranges. The prototype hides setup behind a globe button, has no format choice, puts emoji under Effects and buries the highlight colour in a style modal. All three variants share one model; they differ in how the panel is organised.

Round 3: waiting screen ideas (W1–W3) for the ~30 s while captions generate.

Round 2: variant D keeps the prototype tabs, changes the setup and how text shows, and brings back editing a preset of style.

Every mock is clickable. Use the scenario chips above each mock for edge cases; the clip on the right plays the captions with your settings.

  1. Language, format, auto emoji and style before Generate, with a live sample on the clip. All but language stay editable after, live; changing language asks for new captions.
  2. Lines, not words. One row per line with its time range. Click to edit (text selected, Enter saves, Esc cancels), × deletes, + Add line at the playhead.
  3. Highlight colour on the panel: None + 6 swatches.
  4. Presets only: no Edit chip, no style modal. Size, Hide punctuation, AI Shake, AI Supersize, Random rotation live under "More".
  5. Remembered for the next clips: format, emoji, style and highlight (local storage + saved templates).
  6. Regenerate after edits asks first: "Replaces your N edits".

Variant A

Production parity

A setup screen like production, then three tabs: Captions · Style · Settings.

How it works

  • Before: language, Word by word / Full phrases cards, Auto emoji card, 4 styles, Generate.
  • After: Captions = line rows + Add line. Style = preset grid + highlight colour. Settings = format, emoji, size, punctuation, More effects, language + Regenerate.

Pros

  • Same words and order as production: nothing to relearn, easiest to ship.
  • Each tab is short.

Cons

  • Format and emoji move between screens (setup → Settings tab): two places for one setting.
  • Highlight colour is one tab away from the lines you are watching.

Edge cases

  • No speech: empty state with "Try another language" and Regenerate.
  • Split clip: lines grouped by Part 1 / Part 2.
  • 16:9: sample and captions sit low on the wide frame.
  • Returning user: setup prefilled with last clip's settings, said in one line.

Variant B · recommended

One panel, no tabs

The same compact rows before and after generating; only the bottom swaps Generate for the lines.

How it works

  • Style strip on top (All styles opens the full grid in place).
  • Rows, label left (like the Channel stickers card): Language, Format, Emoji, Highlight, Size; "More effects" folds.
  • Lines below. Before generating, that spot is the Generate button.

Pros

  • One place per setting, before and after: the panel never changes shape.
  • Highlight and format are next to the lines; every change shows on the clip right away (fixes C3 by layout).
  • Same row pattern users already have in Channel stickers.

Cons

  • Long panel once there are many lines; settings scroll away (could stick, see question 2).
  • Differs from production's tab names.

Edge cases

  • No speech: rows stay, empty state replaces the lines.
  • Language changed after: "Regenerate in Spanish" button appears in that row; confirm if lines were edited.
  • Split clip, 16:9, returning user: as A.

Variant C

Transcript first, settings as chips

The lines fill the panel; style, format, emoji and highlight are chips in a sticky bar.

How it works

  • Before: one compact card (language · format · emoji on one row) and Generate.
  • After: chip bar Style ▾ · Format ▾ · Emoji · Highlight ▾ · More ▾; each opens a small popover. Emoji toggles in one click.
  • Each line shows its emoji; click it to drop that emoji from that line only.

Pros

  • Most room for fixing words, the most frequent job after generating.
  • Current values readable at a glance in the chips; settings never scroll away.
  • Per-line emoji control without a new screen.

Cons

  • Popovers hide options until opened (style browsing is a click deeper).
  • Per-line emoji is new behaviour to build and store.

Edge cases

  • Emoji off: line emoji hide; turning it on brings back per-line choices.
  • Word by word: the emoji pops with the first word of its line.
  • No speech, split clip, 16:9: as A.

Questions for you

  2. Format and emoji only before generating (production) or changeable after (all variants)?
  3. Tab name: Captions or Auto Captions?
  4. First-time defaults: Full phrases + Auto emoji on (as in your screenshot)?
