Design draft · captions-tab · round 3 · 30 Oct 2026

Waiting while captions generate (~30 s)

Captions take about 30 seconds after "Generate captions". Today the prototype jumps straight to the lines, so there's no waiting screen at all. Here are three ways to fill that time. Each mock runs a real 30-second job. Use Fast (6 s) to skip ahead, and the scenario chips to see it fail or find no speech.

  • Same in every idea: real steps (Preparing audio → Transcribing speech → Timing words → Adding emoji), a time-left estimate, Cancel (back to setup, choices kept), an error state with Try again, and the existing "No speech found" state at the end.
  • The video keeps playing, and a placeholder shows where captions will appear.
Idea W1 · recommended

Progress card, keep choosing

The panel shows a progress card with the steps. Below it, the choices that don't need a new transcript stay editable.

How it works

  • Card: current step, bar, about N s left, 4-step checklist.
  • "While you wait": Show captions as, Auto emoji and Preset of style stay live, and the sample on the video updates.
  • When done, the panel turns into the line rows, and "Captions ready" shows for a moment.

Pros

  • The wait turns into useful time spent picking the look.
  • Closest to the D1 setup you already have, so it's the smallest change.

Cons

  • Language is locked during the wait. Changing it means Cancel, then start again.
  • The user still stays on the Captions tab.
Idea W2

Lines arrive as they're ready

Switches to the Text tab right away. Lines fill in one by one as they're transcribed, so you see results in seconds instead of waiting the full 30.

How it works

  • A sticky progress bar sits on top ("Transcribing · 6 of ~14 lines"). Grey placeholder rows fill in from the top down.
  • The video shows the newest line.
  • Editing unlocks when all lines are in. Effects and Presets stay usable.

Pros

  • Feels the fastest. People can start reading and spotting mistakes early.
  • Shows the AI is working on their words, which builds trust.

Cons

  • Needs the backend to stream partial results. Without that, it's only a fake animation at the end.
  • Lines can't be edited until the end, which can be frustrating.
Idea W3 · polished

Runs in the background

Production's "Making magic…" screen (mascot + "you can continue working" note) on the dark editor, with the job tracked on the rail so people really can leave.

How it works

  • Captions panel: mascot, "Making magic…", "up to 30 seconds depending on the length of your clip", thin bar with time left, info note "You can keep working on your project…", Cancel.
  • Rail: a ring fills around the Captions icon (hover: "Generating captions · 40%"). Done → green dot; failed → red dot.
  • Off the tab: "Captions are ready · 14 lines added to your video · Review". Captions are already on the video ("Captions on"). Failure toast has Try again right there.
  • Export while generating: a dialog asks "Wait, then export" (export starts by itself when ready) or "Export without captions".

Pros

  • No dead time at all. Fits users who add captions plus stickers and music in one session.
  • The same pattern can be reused for other AI jobs.

Cons

  • More moving parts: the rail ring, toasts, and what happens if they export before captions are ready.
  • Easy to miss when it's done (the toast and green dot cover that).

Questions

  • Can the backend send partial transcript results, or only the full result at the end? W2 needs partial results.
  • W3 now asks the user on Export (wait / export without captions). OK, or should it always wait?
  • The mascot is cut out of the production screenshot for this draft. For the prototype, can we get the original artwork file (SVG or transparent PNG)?
  • Is ~30 s an average? A real number for 60 s vs 3 min clips would make the time-left estimate honest.
